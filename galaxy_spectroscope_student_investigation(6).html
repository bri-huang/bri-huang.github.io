Ways to make the activity more collaborative
  1. Jigsaw regions: assign teams to center, inner disk, or outer disk. Each team collects 3-4 emission-region observations, then exchanges data to build a class rotation curve.
  2. Instrument roles: use telescope operator, spectrum reader, recorder, and skeptic/quality checker. Rotate roles after two observations.
  3. Blind compare: two teams observe the same region without seeing each other's estimates. They compare only after recording and discuss differences.
  4. Class board: each team contributes one best data point to a shared table before graphing on paper.
  5. Consensus check: before calculations, teams decide whether the outer disk gas looks slower, faster, or about the same based only on measured wavelengths.

Student worksheet copy

Goal: Use a telescope and spectroscope simulation to observe H-alpha emission from bright ionized hydrogen regions in M31. Record the nearby guide object, distance from center, and estimated observed wavelength.

Data table columns: Pointing Near / Guide Object | Radius from Galaxy Center (kpc) | Wavelength Obs. lambda_obs (nm) | Delta lambda (nm) | Orbital Speed, |v| (km/s)

Student task: choose several bright H-alpha emission regions at different distances from the center. Each region is shown near a named catalog object, but the measured light is from ionized hydrogen gas. Use the ruler on the spectrum to estimate the H-alpha line. Do all calculations, graphing, mass inference, and CER writing on the paper worksheet.

Discussion prompt: After your group has data, compare with another group. Do emission regions farther from the center appear to slow down in the way the simple central-mass model predicts?
